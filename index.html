Overview

Following the success of the inaugural CLRLC-LLMs Workshop at NeurIPS 2025 in Mexico City, we propose a second edition of the workshop at NeurIPS 2026. The workshop continues to explore the role of Large Language Models (LLMs) in shaping the future of multilingual and culturally inclusive artificial intelligence. While LLMs have transformed natural language processing, they remain largely optimized for high-resource and globally dominant languages — leaving low-resource languages and cultures significantly underrepresented in current AI research and development.

The 2026 edition builds on last year's conversations and aims to reframe the field by placing low-resource languages and cultures at the center of AI innovation. We propose to bring together researchers, linguists, developers, and technologists to share insights, challenges, and strategies for developing, evaluating, and deploying LLMs that uphold linguistic diversity and cultural identity across Africa, Asia, the Americas, and beyond.

Through invited talks, paper presentations, and panel discussions, participants will examine the intersections of AI, linguistics, and cultural diversity — promoting innovations that empower underrepresented language communities globally.

Call for Lightning Talks

Pending workshop acceptance, we will invite researchers, practitioners, linguists, and students working on low-resource languages, culturally aware AI, and inclusive language technologies to submit Lightning Talk abstracts to the Centering Low-Resource Languages and Cultures in the Age of Large Language Models (CLRLC-LLMs) Workshop @ NeurIPS 2026.

Lightning Talks are short, focused presentations (5–7 minutes) that showcase ongoing research, new ideas, or visionary directions relevant to the workshop's theme. The session aims to spark interdisciplinary exchange and highlight work advancing culturally grounded AI.

Topics of Interest

  • LLMs for low-resource languages
  • Culturally grounded NLP and multilingual evaluation
  • Dataset creation and benchmarks for African, Indigenous, and marginalized languages
  • Cross-linguistic transfer and model adaptation
  • Ethical, social, and policy dimensions of language technologies
  • Community-based and participatory AI
  • Speech, ASR, and translation in low-resource settings
  • Human–AI interaction for linguistic and cultural inclusivity
  • Cognitive or sociolinguistic perspectives on AI and language
  • Healthcare and biomedical translation for low-resource languages
